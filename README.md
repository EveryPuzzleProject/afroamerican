# The Afro-American's crossword, 1924–25

The Afro-American, the Black weekly of Baltimore, Maryland, ran a crossword every Saturday
from December 13, 1924 to March 28, 1925: sixteen puzzles, at the height of the crossword
craze. This is the Every Puzzle Project's record of that run. Part of the
[Every Puzzle Project](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject).

The puzzle ran as "This Week's Afro's Crossword Puzzle" on the paper's Magazine Page, beside
"Aunt Dilsey's Puzzle Corner". The banner across the top of the page read, every week:

> EDITED BY AFRO'S WOMAN EDITOR—EVERY READER OF THIS PAGE IS INVITED TO CONTRIBUTE TO IT

Readers did. Three of the first ten puzzles are credited to a reader by name and street, and a
fourth was "submitted by the janitor at School No. 100". Solvers sent answers to the Puzzle
Editor for a prize, and the page printed the winner and a list of honorable mentions that ran
to dozens of names.

## The run

| Dates | Puzzles | What they are |
|---|---|---|
| 1924-12-13 to 1925-02-14 | 10 | The paper's own: small hand-ruled grids, four credited to readers, the rest with no credit. Answers two weeks later, as a list of words in the Puzzle Corner. |
| 1925-02-21 to 1925-03-28 | 6 | A different, uniform type, larger grids, and "(Copyright, 1925.)" under each with no owner named. These look bought in. One, on 1925-03-07, is a heart with FEB 14 spelled in its black squares. |

`puzzles.tsv` has one row per puzzle: date, page, heading, the credit and copyright line as
printed, where its answers were seen, and notes.

## What isn't known

- **Who the contributors were.** One is identified: Georgeanna McMechen of 2007 McCulloh
  Street (1925-02-14) was one of the four daughters of George W. F. McMechen, the Baltimore
  lawyer, and his wife Mildred (looked up by Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-11-19). A "Georgia
  McMechen" among the solvers' honorable mentions on 1924-12-27 is probably her. A. B. Koger
  and John E. Cole Jr. are not yet identified.
- **Who the "woman editor" was.** The page doesn't name her, and neither does this record yet.
- **Who made the six uncredited home-made puzzles**: the editor, "Aunt Dilsey", or readers
  whose names weren't printed.
- **Where the six copyrighted puzzles came from.** Alex DeJarnatt takes the heart-shaped one
  for the New York World's February 14 puzzle; it hasn't been compared with a copy of the
  World's. All six share a typeface and the same copyright line, so one source for all six is
  likely, but none is named.
- **Whether December 13 was the first.** The puzzle answered on that page from the week before
  is a word rhyme, not a crossword, which points to yes. The December 6 page hasn't been looked at.
- **Why it stopped.** No crossword was found after March 1925 (Alex DeJarnatt, searching the
  scans, 2026-11-19). A search finds headings, so a puzzle under another heading could be missed.

`wanted.tsv` lists the pages that would settle these.

## Status (2026-11-19)

All sixteen puzzle pages are saved. Nothing has been read yet: the next step is the OCR
harvest ([xword-ocr](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/xword-ocr)), then review on the
[review site](https://blitz.xwordapp.com/review/). Clues and answers are not typed in here;
puzzle content comes from that pipeline, so nothing typed by hand gets in silently.

## Images

Page images don't go in this repo. They live in the private twin `afroamerican-scans`. The
pages are from 1924–25 and out of copyright in the US; whether to publish them is a separate
decision.
